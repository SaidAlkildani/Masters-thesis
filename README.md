# Masters-thesis
This is a repository of my master's thesis under the title of "Identifying Cross-organ Pan-fibrotic Disease Signatures in their Cellular Context" conducted with the Intelligent Imaging group at the BIH, Berlin. <be>


This repository was created after the project's completion to maintain the code. Each folder has a README file that explains the purpose of the notebooks. 

## Abstract

Fibrosis, a dysregulated wound-healing process that leads to the overproduction of extracellular matrix (ECM) proteins, represents a major clinical burden due to its prevalence in fibrotic diseases and as collateral damage from non-fibrotic diseases. In some conditions, fibrosis is irreversible and, at best, managed with anti-fibrotic drugs and/or surgical intervention. This project utilizes publicly available single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data from six fibrotic diseases — interstitial lung diseases, chronic kidney disease, chronic pancreatitis, endometriosis, keloids, and cirrhosis — to identify pan-fibrosis molecular programs. By integrating nine datasets, we aimed to determine whether mesenchymal-immune molecular programs are conserved across these diseases. Our findings revealed a conserved myofibroblast cluster, as well as newly appearing clusters of proliferating myeloid and lymphoid cells across all datasets. Myofibroblasts, endothelial cells, pericytes, and T cells were significantly increased in fibrotic diseases. Cell-cell interaction analysis identified conserved crosstalk of profibrotic gene signatures across all diseases from fibroblasts and/or myofibroblasts to macrophages and/or proliferating myeloid cells. Overall, this study establishes a foundation for exploring pan-fibrotic molecular mechanisms by providing a detailed workflow for integrating scRNA-seq datasets that handles over one million cells, highlighting the potential of scRNA-seq integrative analyses, and identifying molecular candidates for targeted pan-fibrotic therapeutics.
